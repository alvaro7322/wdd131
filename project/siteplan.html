Site Name

Site Name: 322 Archive

Optional Domain Availability: 322archive.gg

Description: I chose the name "322 Archive" because it combines "322"—the most famous numeric meme and competitive scandal in Dota 2 history—with "Archive", which signals to visitors that the site is an educational repository documenting match-fixing incidents and their severe historical impact on South American esports.

Site Purpose

The site provides an educational and historical repository about match-fixing in Dota 2 by offering a chronological timeline of the original 2013 "322" incident, detailed case studies of South American banned teams like Elite Wolves, and informative resources on competitive integrity regulations for aspiring pro players.

Scenarios

  • Why does the Dota 2 community and Twitch chat constantly use the term "322" whenever a player makes a mistake, and what is the original story behind that number?
  • Which South American teams and players were permanently banned by Valve for match-fixing, when did it happen, and how did those bans impact the growth of esports in the region?

Color Schema

The site's color palette is directly inspired by the dark, dramatic aesthetic of Dota 2 and esports interfaces:

  • Primary Background (#121212): A dark charcoal gray used for the main body background to give a gaming feel and reduce eye strain.
  • Secondary Container (#1e1e1e): A dark gray used for section cards, headers, and content blocks to create contrast and structure.
  • Accent Red (#af2b2b): Inspired by the iconic Dota 2 logo crimson red. Used for main headings (H1, H2), borders, and emphasis on bans/warnings.
  • Accent Gold (#d4a359): Inspired by the Aegis gold color. Used for subheadings (H3), important dates in timelines, key terms, and links.
  • Text Main (#e0e0e0): An off-white color used for all body text, paragraphs, and list items to ensure high legibility.

Typography

The site uses three distinct web fonts imported from Google Fonts to structure hierarchy and highlight historical quotes from the competitive scene:

  • Heading Font — Montserrat (Bold / Extra-Bold 800): Used for main titles (H1, H2, H3). A strong, geometric sans-serif that delivers high visual impact suited for esports.
  • Casual Text Font — Open Sans (Regular 400): Used for all standard body copy, paragraphs, scenario descriptions, and list items to guarantee clean legibility across devices.
  • Quote Font — Cinzel (Italic 600): Used specifically for player quotes, official Valve statements, and iconic casters' declarations (rendered inside <blockquote> elements) to give them a dramatic and historical tone.
