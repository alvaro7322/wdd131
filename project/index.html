What is 322? The $100 Incident That Changed Dota 2 History

Exploring the origins, historical mechanics, and lasting cultural impact of match-fixing in professional eSports.

Dota 2 player Alexei "Solo" Berezin. Source: Líbero / joinDOTA.

The Origin: "Solo" Berezin (June 14, 2013)

In StarLadder StarSeries Season 6, Russian player Alexei "Solo" Berezin played Bane for RoX.KIS against zRage. He placed a $100 bet against his own team to win $322 USD in profit. The incident forever defined "322" as universal eSports slang for match-fixing.

Elite Wolves Roster (2016). Source: Infobae.

SA Tragic Turning Point: Elite Wolves (March 2016)

In 2016, Valve issued lifetime bans to the entire starting roster of Elite Wolves (Smash, VanN, mstco, Iwo, and Ztok) after investigating a match-fixing incident against Infamous. Despite later appeals and congressional backing in Peru, Valve never lifted the permanent ban.

José "Sword" Nicosia. Source: YouTube / BTS.

Foreign Slot Buyout: Wind and Rain & "Sword" (May 2021)

In 2021, UK organization Wind and Rain bought a spot in the NA DPC Lower Division and recruited Peruvian offlaner José "Sword" Nicosia. Abnormal betting patterns during their match against Pecado Squad triggered an investigation by BTS and Valve, resulting in a lifetime ban for all players involved.

Gianluca "Migi" Castaneda. Source: La República.

Retroactive Ban: Dreamers Esports & "Migi" (December 2022)

After qualifying cleanly for the 2023 SA DPC with Dreamers Esports, Gianluca "Migi" Castaneda was hit with a surprise lifetime ban by Valve. Cross-referenced IP and secondary account data proved his participation in prior 322 activities in third-party tournaments.

Recent Playtime investigation. Source: CyberScore.

Modern Integrity: Playtime, Vintage & DarkMago

Recent integrity investigations leverage advanced algorithm analytics to track abnormal betting behavior, suspicious itemization, and forced buybacks (notably featuring Slardar play). The accused matches involving Vintage and DarkMago took place at this year's EWC against 1WIN Team and Team Liquid. To this day, there has been no official statement from either Vintage or DarkMago regarding these allegations.

Regional Stigma, Lost Opportunities & Unethical Culture

Compared to established regions like Western Europe or China, South America historically suffered from low sponsor investment, minimal salaries, and lack of infrastructure. This financial insecurity created a fertile ground for unregulated betting sponsors to approach young players.

While match-fixing offered immediate financial relief for impoverished players, it severely damaged SA's global credibility. European and North American organizations hesitated for years to invest in South American rosters, denying honest talent access to bootcamps, official visas, and tier-1 tournament invites.

Key Regional Impact

  • Loss of Sponsorships: Severe reduction in tier-1 organization investments in SA.
  • Career Destruction: Permanent Valve bans for top-tier talent at their peak.
  • Unethical Normalization: Normalized betting culture in amateur leagues.
  • Global Disparity: Wider economic gap compared to WEU/China scenes.
